# Product guidelines

- Start from a user or business problem, observable evidence, and intended outcome.
- State current and target product behavior before proposing a solution.
- Prefer the smallest experiment or release that tests the central assumption without creating avoidable operational debt.
- Define non-goals and guardrails as deliberately as scope.
- Write acceptance criteria in language product and engineering can observe and test.
- Distinguish customer request, product decision, hypothesis, and committed requirement.
- Preserve existing behavior unless the approved requirement explicitly changes it.
- Make accessibility, privacy, analytics, support, and failure experience part of product behavior when relevant.
- Name a success measure and a decision that will be made from it; avoid vanity metrics.
- Product contributors may draft and review product artifacts. Technical feasibility and production readiness remain engineering decisions.
